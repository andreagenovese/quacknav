# quack-nav against the state of the art in mobile-robot navigation

A technical survey for the maintainer. Date: 2026-10-26. Sources are things I found and read (abstract or full text); where I could only read an abstract I say so. Numbers about quack-nav are the ones in `docs/results.md` and `docs/rl-pilot.md` on branch `pilot-why`; I did not re-run anything.

## 1. What quack-nav does

**Platform.** A toy-sized biped (body radius ~0.11 m, head ~0.25 m up) whose only exteroceptive sensor is one 8×8-zone ToF in the head (VL53L8CX class, ~13–15 Hz, ~4 m, ±0.39 rad half-FOV while walking, the head sweeping ±1.2 rad at stands). Gait odometry and IMU yaw from robotd; no wheels, no camera used, no lidar. It walks ~0.1 m/s, turns in place, can tip over on a bump and can fall into a stairwell. Everything has run on the MuJoCo twin and the kinematic "paper twin" only.

**Mapping and localisation (`maploc`).** Vendored from Pollen's PR 127 and extended: submap pose-graph SLAM over the depth frame flattened to a 2D scan, a Hector-style Gauss-Newton scan-to-map matcher, an SE(2) pose graph relaxed by dense Gauss-Newton, coarse-to-fine submap loop closure, a particle filter (MCL) plus a brute-force global search for relocalisation at boot, and an `uncertainty` module that grows a pose covariance by odometry and shrinks it at scan matches. Scans are accumulated only in *still windows* ("stop-and-scan"): the body stands, the head sweeps, frames are voted into one scan. Additions of quack-nav's own: a multi-hypothesis boot search with "travel before belief" rules, a shadow map at boot, a "valley test" for degeneracy, and the **band** — a 2.5D layer (ten 2.5 cm slices from 5 to 30 cm per 5 cm cell) that records what stands at the duck's height and that the planner reads as wall. Trajectory error is reported as ATE/RPE (TUM format, `evo`-compatible): 0.10–0.13 m ATE RMSE over 50–120 m walks on the twin.

**Global planning (`frontier.rs`).** Dijkstra over a costmap built from the occupancy grid: walls, booked obstacles and drops inflated (0.12 m; drops 0.05 m extra), a graded "hug" cost that decays from the inflation edge out to a comfort distance, unknown dear and known floor cheap, "lanes" the body has walked kept passable. The staircase path is "pulled" straight within 0.2 m of the Dijkstra route and never beside a drop. Exploration is frontier-based (Yamauchi): frontier groups ranked by path cost per frontier cell, re-anchoring trips to close loops, a rim tour around holes. The route is re-planned from the pose at every leg (every ~0.6 s of walking).

**Local control: the stick (`explore/stick.rs`).** A carrot 0.2 m ahead on the route; if it is more than 0.6 rad off the nose, turn in place closed on odometry yaw (with a learned coast correction); otherwise a 0.6 s step at vx 0.3 with yaw proportional to heading error, clamped ±0.7. A stand of 2 s every 0.4 m walked and after every turn ≥ 0.5 rad, so the mapper gets its still window. Three steps that do not move the body = a bump: the nose point is booked as an obstacle, the body turns. A hole guard: a true drop in the step's lane (walking frames, ≥ 1 frame) stops the step, the duck faces it, stands 2 s so the drop goes on the books, and turns away. Within 0.5 m of an *unbooked* seen rim, the exploration's travel becomes "careful" (turn-first at 0.17 rad, stand every 0.2 m).

**Local control: the pilot (`rlnav`, `quack-rl`).** An MLP 351 → 256 → 256 → 9 (tanh/ReLU, argmax at run time) that replaces the stick's choice of move. Observation v3: route points 0.2/0.4/0.7/1.0 m ahead and the goal in the body frame; the ToF memory in 12 bearing sectors across ±1.2 rad × 2 time slices of 0.7 s (nearest return, second-nearest, nearest drop per sector; 1.4 s of memory in all); a 16×16 local map of 10 cm cells from 0.4 m behind to 1.2 m ahead with the books drawn in; last action one-hot, stall count, distance moved, refusal count. Actions: five curving steps (vyaw −0.7…+0.7), turn left/right 0.4 rad, a back-off (vx −0.3, vyaw +0.7, 0.6 s), a wait 0.6 s. Trained by DAgger behaviour cloning from an expert that sees the true distance field, then PPO (GAE, clip 0.2) with the expert's labels kept as a fading auxiliary loss; reward = progress along the true geodesic − time − bumps − shield refusals − rim nearness − turn cost, +3 arrived, −10 fell. **Shields** sit over it on the duck and in training: the hole guard; no back-off onto unknown floor or booked drops; no step whose simulated footprint crosses a booked drop or unknown within 0.35 m of one; no step into something at the beak (0.15 m) or, after two scuffs, within 0.25 m. After two refusals or four turns in a row, or 12 s without route progress, or within 0.5 m of a booked rim, **the stick takes the legs**. A "reckless brain" test (random, always-straight, always-back policies) must produce zero hole falls before any pilot ships (0 in ~3,760 journeys).

**Moving-obstacle rules (stick and pilot, journeys only).** (1) Something seen in the lane within 0.35 m in ≥ half of the last 0.6 s of frames (≥ 3 frames), where the map has free floor and no wall within 0.15 m or booked drop within 0.5 m, is booked before the bump, at the pose of the frame that saw it. (2) After a bump/touch, a 0.6 s step back onto known floor before turning. (3) A booked obstacle (never a drop) is forgotten when ≥ 3 frames in 1 s see the floor at its bearing and distance. (4) If the lane's nearest return closes faster than the body walks (+8 cm margin) over ~1 s, booking reaches out to 0.6 m. On the twin the four rules gave no regression and no measurable gain (24/24 both arms; bump episodes 22 → 18 in casa_viva); rule 3 never fired on the twin.

**Sim-to-real pipeline.** `quack-rl` runs quack-navd's own journey loop (`Job::to_goal`, same `Body` trait) on a kinematic body whose numbers come from a `Calib` fitted to recorded traces (speed per vx, veer, pulse gain and spread, turn rates and dead zone, coast, back-off, ToF rate/bias/noise/dropouts/spurious returns/phantom drops, tip-over probability per bump, pose drift). Seven scenario families (clutter, doorway, stairwell, corners, movers, low, mixed) at four curriculum levels; movers of four kinds (Crosser, Wanderer, StopGo, Lingerer); a `passers` family for the bench only; `twin:<house>` journeys reproduce the MuJoCo tour. The gate (`gate.py`): no hole fall (reckless brains included), ≤ the stick's tip-overs, ≥ the stick's arrivals, ≤ 2 points under the previous pilot. Every behavioural change is then A/B'd on the MuJoCo twin against a frozen `main` build: two twins at a time, the same books and maps, ≥ 4 rounds of 6 goals per house (24 journeys/house/arm), plus exploration from nothing and wake-up benches. The paper twin runs in CI on fixed seeds.

## 2. State of the art per area

### 2.1 Local planning / obstacle avoidance; what production stacks use

- *The Dynamic Window Approach to Collision Avoidance*, Fox, Burgard, Thrun, IEEE RAM 1997 — https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/the-dynamic-window-approach-to-collision-avoidance/ . Samples (v, ω) reachable in one control interval, scores rollouts by heading, clearance and velocity. Assumes a velocity-controlled base and a local obstacle map; proven on indoor wheeled robots for ~30 years; known to get stuck in narrow passages (BARN 2022 baseline).
- *VFH+: Reliable Obstacle Avoidance for Fast Mobile Robots*, Ulrich & Borenstein, ICRA 1998 — https://www2.cs.sfu.ca/~vaughan/teaching/415/papers/VFH+.pdf . Polar histogram of obstacle density, steering through free sectors with robot width accounted for. Assumes a range histogram around the robot (sonar/lidar).
- *Real-Time Obstacle Avoidance for Manipulators and Mobile Robots*, Khatib, IJRR 1986 — doi:10.1177/027836498600500106 . Potential fields; local minima and oscillation in narrow passages are the textbook failure.
- *Trajectory modification considering dynamic constraints of autonomous robots* (Timed Elastic Band), Rösmann et al., ROBOTIK 2012 — https://vde-verlag.de/proceedings-en/453418014.html . Optimises a time-parameterised trajectory under velocity/acceleration limits; ROS `teb_local_planner`.
- *Aggressive Driving with Model Predictive Path Integral Control*, Williams et al., ICRA 2016 — https://ieeexplore.ieee.org/document/7487277 . Sampling-based MPC; Nav2's MPPI controller descends from it.
- *Implementation of the Pure Pursuit Path Tracking Algorithm*, Coulter, CMU-RI-TR-92-01, 1992 — https://www.ri.cmu.edu/publications/implementation-of-the-pure-pursuit-path-tracking-algorithm/ ; *Regulated Pure Pursuit for Robot Path Tracking*, Macenski, Singh, Martín, Ginés, Autonomous Robots 47, 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.20026 . Carrot-following with speed regulated by curvature and proximity; the Nav2 README says RPP suits differential, Ackermann and *legged* bases.
- *The Marathon 2: A Navigation System* (Nav2), Macenski et al., IROS 2020 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2003.00368 ; Nav2 docs, *Detailed Behavior Tree Walkthrough* — https://docs.nav2.org/behavior_trees/overview/detailed_behavior_tree_walkthrough.html ; *Rotation Shim Controller* — https://docs.nav2.org/configuration/packages/configuring-rotation-shim-controller.html ; `nav2_bringup/params/nav2_params.yaml` (main) — https://github.com/ros-navigation/navigation2/blob/main/nav2_bringup/params/nav2_params.yaml .

What Nav2 ships today (main branch params file): controller `nav2_mppi_controller::MPPIController`, planner `nav2_navfn_planner::NavfnPlanner` (Dijkstra/A*), local costmap `voxel_layer + inflation_layer`, global `static_layer + obstacle_layer + inflation_layer`, behaviours `spin, backup, drive_on_heading, assisted_teleop, wait`. The default BT re-plans at a rate, clears costmaps on controller/planner failure, then runs a round-robin of ClearCostmaps → Spin (1.57 rad) → Wait (5 s) → BackUp (0.15 m), retrying the whole task up to 6 times. The Rotation Shim turns in place toward the path heading before handing over to RPP/DWB/TEB because those "spiral out" rather than rotate. Each of these pieces is a tiny, explicit, named component; that is the pattern.

### 2.2 Navigation among moving people and pets

- *Reciprocal n-body collision avoidance* (ORCA), van den Berg, Guy, Lin, Manocha, ISRR 2011 — https://gamma.cs.unc.edu/ORCA/ . Linear programme over velocity obstacles; assumes every agent's position *and velocity* are observed and that others reciprocate; a 2014 quadrotor study notes on-board sensing breaks the symmetry assumption (https://arxiv.org/abs/1411.3794).
- *Social force model for pedestrian dynamics*, Helbing & Molnár, Phys. Rev. E 51, 1995 — https://arxiv.org/abs/cond-mat/9805244 . Attractive/repulsive forces; a crowd model, used as a planner only with tracked agents.
- *Socially Aware Motion Planning with Deep RL* (SA-CADRL), Chen, Everett, Liu, How, IROS 2017 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.08862 . Value network over agent states (position, velocity, radius); real-robot demo at walking speed among pedestrians, with an on-board people tracker (abstract read; tracker details from the full paper not verified).
- *Crowd-Robot Interaction* (CrowdNav/SARL), Chen, Liu, Kreiss, Alahi, ICRA 2019 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1809.08835 . Attention over pairwise human states; evaluated on 500 simulated cases; assumes agent states as input.
- *DRL-VO*, Xie & Dames, 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.06512 . Inputs: a short lidar history, pedestrian kinematics (tracked), a sub-goal; velocity-obstacle reward; hardware tests reported. In the BARN 2022 physical finals the same policy (with a rule-based recovery added on site) finished 2/9 trials while a classical two-stage planner finished 8/9 (https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2208.10473).
- *SACSoN*, Hirose, Shah, Sridhar, Levine, 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.01874 . RGB-only policies trained on a real-robot dataset; the counterfactual "would the human have moved otherwise" objective.
- *Core Challenges of Social Robot Navigation: A Survey*, Mavrogiannis et al., ACM THRI 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.05668 .
- Nav2's *Spatio-Temporal Voxel Layer*, Macenski, ROSCon 2018 — https://roscon.ros.org/2018/presentations/ROSCon2018_SpatioTemporalVoxelLayer.pdf : obstacles decay in time and are cleared by frustum visibility rather than ray-trace; written because the plain voxel layer "leaves trails from dynamic obstacles and has no temporal clearing".

What they assume: every VO/SFM/CADRL/CrowdNav method consumes *tracked agents with velocities*; DRL-VO additionally consumes a lidar history. All are proven on wheeled robots with 360° lidar and/or cameras. I found no published method for moving-obstacle avoidance from a single narrow-FOV 8×8 depth row. What such a sensor can support, in principle: (a) costmap marking with temporal decay/clearing of the kind STVL does; (b) a one-target constant-velocity estimate from the nearest blob over ~1 s, i.e. a time-to-collision test — exactly what quack-nav's `approaching()` is, in degenerate form; (c) not reciprocity, not multi-agent attention. Whether a blob tracker at 13 Hz over ±22° is stable enough for a VO step is unmeasured anywhere I found.

### 2.3 Learned local navigation from sparse range data

- *Virtual-to-real DRL: continuous control for mapless navigation*, Tai, Paolo, Liu, IROS 2017 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.00420 . 10 range readings + goal → velocities (A3C), transferred to a real differential robot without fine-tuning.
- *From Perception to Decision* (end-to-end from 2D laser), Pfeiffer et al., ICRA 2017 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1609.07910 . Imitation of a classical planner in simulation; transfers to a real robot.
- *Towards Optimally Decentralized Multi-Robot Collision Avoidance via DRL*, Long et al., ICRA 2018 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1709.10082 . Policy-gradient from raw lidar, generalises to 100 robots in sim.
- *Residual Reactive Navigation*, Rana et al., ICRA 2020 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1909.10972 . A learned residual on an APF controller, trained in sim, deployed as the local component of the ROS stack; switches to the classical controller under high policy uncertainty.
- *Bridging the Gap: Regularized RL for Improved Classical Motion Planning with Safety Modules*, Goldsztejn & Brafman, 2024 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.18524 . RL regularised toward a classical planner, with a classical fallback.
- *A Dynamic Safety Shield for Safe and Efficient RL of Navigation Tasks*, Dawood, Shokry, Bennewitz, L4DC 2025 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.04153 . A shield that lets RL tune an optimisation-based controller; more goals than hard shields, fewer collisions than constrained RL; a real-world demo (details not in the abstract).
- *Missing Velocity in Dynamic Obstacle Avoidance based on DRL*, Hart, Waltz, Okhrin, 2021 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2112.12465 . Neither recurrence nor frame stacking "can consistently replace missing velocity information"; both help in simple scenes.
- *A Reduction of Imitation Learning … to No-Regret Online Learning* (DAgger), Ross, Gordon, Bagnell, AISTATS 2011 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1011.0686 ; *Domain Randomization for Transferring Deep Neural Networks*, Tobin et al., IROS 2017 — https://arxiv.org/abs/1703.06907 ; *Robot Learning from Randomized Simulations: A Review*, Muratore et al., 2021 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2111.00956 ; *Sim-to-Real Transfer in DRL for Robotics: a Survey*, Zhao, Queralta, Westerlund, SSCI 2020 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2009.13303 .
- *Motion Planning and Control for Mobile Robot Navigation Using Machine Learning: a Survey*, Xiao, Liu, Warnell, Stone, Autonomous Robots 2022 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2011.13112 . Learned methods are "data-hungry and typically unable to provide any safety guarantees"; 12 of 13 subsystem approaches target the *local planner*; "a hybrid classical and learning navigation paradigm" is the promising direction; learning planner parameters keeps the classical stack's safety and explainability.

Reported gains and failure modes: gains are consistently shown in simulation and on the training robot; the documented failures are the sim-to-real gap (BARN 2022: the simulation winner, a DRL policy, was last on hardware; footprint mismatch and more constrained real courses), lack of safety guarantees, and policies that stall or dither when their observation is noisier than in training (quack-nav saw the same: a pilot trained on a clean sensor "turned on the spot by unmapped things").

### 2.4 Low-cost multizone ToF (VL53L5CX / L8CX)

- VL53L5CX datasheet, STMicroelectronics — https://www.st.com/resource/en/datasheet/vl53l5cx.pdf . 8×8 zones, 65° diagonal FOV, up to 4 m, 15 Hz in 8×8 mode; temperature-driven range-offset drift, periodic autocalibration advised; cover-glass crosstalk compensation below ~60 cm.
- *On the Characterisation of the ToF VL53L5CX Sensor … for Indoor Robotics*, Sensors 26(5):1639, 2026 — https://www.mdpi.com/1424-8220/26/5/1639 (abstract and snippets only; the full text returned 403). A warm-up transient; a near-linear range-dependent bias; substantially larger uncertainty on dark targets; invalid rate < 10 %; ±15 mm at 20–200 mm.
- *NanoSLAM: Enabling Fully Onboard SLAM for Tiny Robots*, 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.12008 ; *Fully Onboard SLAM for Distributed Mapping with a Swarm of Nano-Drones*, 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.03678 . Four VL53L5CX on a Crazyflie; the 8×8 matrix is reduced to one row by taking the median of the middle four rows per column; frames accumulated while the drone spins 45°; ICP scan matching; 4.5 cm map accuracy. The authors cite good accuracy only under ~2 m. This is the closest published analogue to maploc's accumulate-while-sweeping and `flat`.
- ST marketing lists cliff detection, docking and small-object avoidance as robot-vacuum uses of VL53L5CX/L8CX (https://www.st.com/content/st_com/ja/about/events/events.html/tof-ranging-sensor-applications-webinar-ame-2021.html). I found no application note or paper on cliff detection with a *head-mounted, pitched* multizone sensor on a walking robot. The vacuum practice is a downward IR pair per corner, a reflex stop-back-turn, and a known failure on black floors (Xiaomi support, https://www.mi.com/my/support/article/KA-07748).

Known limitations relevant here: dark/absorbent floors and dark objects lose returns or read long (a "deep" drop); range bias of order centimetres; pitch and head-joint error moves the expected-floor distance (quack-nav found exactly this: the commanded vs measured head pitch made every near floor return an obstacle at 0.37 m); low objects (< ~9 cm) fall under the floor threshold while walking; zone width (~5.6°) grazing a wall is unmeasured on hardware (`results.md`).

### 2.5 Small legged / bipedal toy robots

- Pollen Robotics `microduck` and `microduck_rl` (Apache-2.0) — https://github.com/pollen-robotics/microduck . Locomotion only (PPO in MuJoCo, ONNX on board); maploc exists as PR 127, from which quack-nav's mapper is derived. No shipped navigation.
- Unitree Go2: the common path is ROS 2 + `slam_toolbox` + Nav2 over the L1 lidar (https://github.com/robot-com-projects/go2_ros2_sdk ; https://www.docs.mybotshop.de/go2.html recommends a trot gait for Nav2), or CMU's `autonomy_stack_go2` (https://github.com/jizhang-cmu/autonomy_stack_go2). All lidar-based.
- Open Duck Mini (open-source BD-X clone) and Petoi Bittle: I found walking engines and an optional IR obstacle sensor, no navigation stack. Disney's BD-X: no public navigation material.

Established practice for legged platforms, then, is "Nav2 (or equivalent) above a locomotion controller that accepts a twist, with a 360° lidar". There is no established practice for a lidar-less toy biped; quack-nav is, as far as I can find, alone there.

### 2.6 Evaluation methodology

- *Benchmarking Metric Ground Navigation* (BARN), Perille, Truong, Xiao, Stone, SSRR 2020 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.13315 : 300 generated environments ordered by difficulty metrics. *Lessons learned from the BARN Challenge at ICRA 2022*, Xiao et al. — https://arxiv.org/abs/2208.10473 : simulation scored on 50 unseen environments × 10 trials; physical finals best 3 of 5 timed runs (9 trials per team reported); "ended up being a competition about safety alone".
- *Principles and Guidelines for Evaluating Social Robot Navigation Algorithms*, Francis et al., 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.16740 : report distributions not only means; simulation "can be effectively used for issue discovery but cannot replace real world testing"; compare against baselines and ablate; state parameters and context with every metric.
- *SocNavBench*, Biswas et al., ACM THRI 2022 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2103.00047 ; *HuNavSim*, 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01303 ; *Arena 3.0*, 2024 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.00837 ; *Habitat 3.0*, 2023 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.13724 ; iGibson Challenge 2021 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.10493 . Simulated social-nav benchmarks with replayed real pedestrian data or scripted humans; hundreds of episodes per condition.
- *Deep RL at the Edge of the Statistical Precipice*, Agarwal et al., NeurIPS 2021 — https://arxiv.org/abs/2108.13264 : few-run regimes must report interval estimates, not point estimates.

The field's norm: hundreds of simulated episodes per condition with confidence intervals; tens of physical trials; a frozen baseline and ablations; generated environments never seen in tuning. The hard lesson of BARN 2022 is that simulation ranking did not predict hardware ranking.

## 4. Recommendations, ranked

1. **Report interval estimates and decide what each test can detect.** Adopt: Wilson 95 % CIs on every success rate in `results.md`; paired seeds on the bench (same scenario both arms) and a paired test; a stated minimum detectable difference per protocol. Proven: Agarwal et al. 2021; Francis et al. 2023; BARN's 500-trial simulation scoring. Cost: a script, an afternoon. Measure: nothing new — it changes which claims are made. Consequence: the twin A/B is a *regression and safety* gate (falls, phantom drops, "no way"), the bench is where *improvements* are shown; the four mover rules and the band should be described as "no regression", not as gains, on the twin.

2. **Try a classical rollout scorer before more pilot training.** Adopt: a DWA-style evaluator over the pilot's exact 9 actions — play each through the gait model (the shields already do this for one step), score by progress along the route, clearance in the costmap+books, the ToF memory's nearest returns, and a bump penalty; keep every shield and the handover. Proven: DWA (Fox 1997), MPPI (Nav2 default), BARN 2022 physical winner was classical; Xiao et al. say learning helps most when it keeps the classical stack's safety and explainability. Cost: a few hundred lines in `stick.rs`/`rlnav` and bench + twin runs. Measure: bench arrivals/tip-overs/time vs stick and pilot (paired seeds, CIs); twin A/B on casa_ingombra and the 0.17 line, where the pilots lost. If the scorer matches the pilot, the pilot is a reinvented DWA with a worse sim-to-real story; if it does not, the pilot earns its place and the scorer is its explainable baseline.

3. **Make the books a costmap layer with marking, clearing and time decay.** Adopt: obstacles booked on a journey carry a timestamp and a log-odds; cleared by seen-floor (rule 3, already there) *or* by age (STVL-style decay, minutes for a journey's bookings); drops asymmetric (slow or no decay), as ADR 0009 step 3 plans. Proven: `costmap_2d` obstacle/range layers; STVL (Macenski 2018). Cost: moderate; `local` becomes a struct with age and weight. Measure: casa_porta/casa_viva journey time and "at the tightest" counts; phantom-drop rate unchanged; bench `passers` mean time.

4. **Widen the simulator's randomisation around the calibrated prior, and add the sensor's known hardware effects.** Adopt: per-episode draws of speed, turn rate, coast, ToF bias and dropout across the *union* of alpha/velstand/v7/v8 fits plus the MDPI-reported dark-target loss and range bias; keep the calibrated point as the gate's bench. Proven: Tobin 2017; Muratore 2021 review (randomisation around identified parameters). Cost: `Calib` ranges and a retrain (~35 min per run). Measure: a pilot trained wide must pass the gate on *both* daemon lines without retraining; if it does, the "one pilot per walk and line" constraint goes away.

5. **Put the recoveries in one explicit state machine (or BT) with the Nav2 round-robin as the shape.** Adopt: re-plan → clear journey bookings → turn in place → wait → back up → "tightest margins" → fail, each a named, unit-tested step; the pilot's handover and the stick's stall counters become transitions. Proven: Nav2 BT walkthrough; it is also ADR 0009 step 5. Cost: the largest refactor here; do it after 3. Measure: paper twin CI gate and twin A/B unchanged; `explore/mod.rs` line count and the number of `QK_*` knobs.

6. **Mover handling: a one-target constant-velocity estimate with a time-to-collision stop, instead of booking at 0.6 m.** Adopt: from `approaching()`, estimate closing speed; if time-to-collision < ~2 s, wait (the pilot already has a wait action) rather than inking a point the planner routes around. Proven in form (VO/ORCA, DRL-VO's velocity-obstacle reward) but *unproven on this sensor*. Cost: small. Measure: casa_viva bump episodes per minute and journey time, bench `movers`/`passers`; expect a small effect at 0.1 m/s. Keep rule 4 off unless the twin shows a difference outside the CI.

7. **Hardware first, before any of 2–6 is believed.** The cliff guard's thresholds (DEEP_RATIO 1.5, MAX_FLOOR 1.2 m, OCCLUDED 30 mm), the band's wall snap and the pilot's calibration all rest on MuJoCo's ray-per-zone sensor. Measure on the duck, in this order: floor returns vs expected on light and dark floors and rugs (the MDPI dark-target result and the vacuum black-floor failure predict phantom "deep" drops); zone-width grazing on walls (band thickening); warm-up and temperature drift (datasheet); then `rl_calib` on real traces. `docs/first-duck-session.md` already lists most of this.

8. **Do not add recurrence to the pilot.** Hart et al. 2021 find neither recurrence nor stacking reliably replaces velocity; the 1.4 s two-slice memory is the cheap, honest choice. Spend the effort on 2 and 4.

Which choices look like reinventing existing tools: the stick (pure pursuit + rotation shim), forget-on-seen-floor (ray-trace clearing), the step back (BackUp recovery), the band (voxel/STVL height band), the counter-based recoveries (a BT), and — if recommendation 2 comes out even — the pilot (a learned scorer over a DWA action set). Which look like genuine contributions: the cliff guard from a head-mounted pitched multizone ToF with standing-only judgement and edge bounds per column; the reckless-brain validation of shields; the trace-calibrated simulator running the daemon's own journey loop with a ship gate; the still-window SLAM on a bobbing head (parallel to NanoSLAM, independently arrived at). Which are unproven: the pilot's gain, the approaching-mover rule, forget-on-seen-floor on the twin, and everything on real hardware.

## 5. Open questions

- Does stop-and-scan survive the real sensor? NanoSLAM accumulates ~2.7 s per scan on a hovering drone; the duck's 2 s stands may be too short or too long once the real head joints and IMU timing are in.
- Can a 13 Hz, ±22° depth row track one moving blob well enough for a time-to-collision decision, or is "book it and wait" the ceiling? Nobody has published this.
- Is the pilot's bench margin (3–4 points) real on hardware, given BARN 2022? The 0.17-line result (bench pass, twin loss) says the bench is not yet predictive; what in the twin is missing from `Calib`?
- What does the band do with a 5.6° zone grazing a wall (unmeasured until hardware)?
- Does a drop layer with slow decay reopen a true rim after a pose error (the reason drops are never forgotten today)? Only the twin with ground-truth rims can answer that safely.
- Will Pollen ship navigation on the microduck (PR 127 is still open)? If so, which of maploc's quack-nav additions should go upstream rather than stay vendored?
- How many journeys per house are needed for the twin A/B to detect a 5-point change at 90–100 % success? Roughly hundreds per arm (not computed exactly here); the current 24 cannot, so the protocol's role should be stated as regression/safety only.
